March 14, 2019

June 7, 2019

Brie is back on active deployment. She walks with a slight limp now that gets worse when it's cold. She's 17. She told me she's been a Keeper since she was 10 — seven years.

Seven years.

I asked her if she ever thought about what she'd do after. She looked at me like I'd said something in a language she didn't understand. "After what?" she said.

There is no after. The Radiance doesn't let go. I knew that, I've always known that, but hearing Brie say it was different. She said it the way you'd say "water is wet" or "the sky is blue." Just a fact. Just the shape of the world.

Seven years. She's been doing this for seven years and she will keep doing it until—

I didn't finish that sentence in my head. I'm not going to finish it here either.

October 3, 2019

Something bad happened and I need to write it down because I can't say it out loud.

We got deployed to a Class II in Arvada. Me, Maya, Brie, and Flicker's first real deployment (she'd done two Class Is before, handled fine). The Fracture was moderate but there were a LOT of Tier 1s, like a swarm. I was running optical cover — cloaking the civilian evacuation — and Maya was doing sweep and Brie was doing barriers and Flicker was up front because her burst ability is good for crowds.

A Tier 2 emerged. We didn't see it until it was close. Flicker was in its path. Brie screamed at her to fall back. Flicker panicked and detonated a burst at point blank range. It staggered the Hollow but it didn't stop it. The Hollow caught her arm.

Hollow contact dissolves organic material.

I was there in three seconds. I hit it with a focused beam and it broke apart. But I was three seconds too late.

Flicker is alive. She lost her arm below the elbow. She is nine years old and she lost her arm below the elbow. The Radiance is healing what it can, which is not enough, which is never enough.

I threw up behind the AKC van after. Agent Morales held my hair. She didn't say "it's okay" because it's not. I appreciate that she didn't lie to me.

Flicker's parents are at the hospital. Her dad keeps asking "how could you let this happen" to anyone in an AKC uniform. Her mom isn't saying anything. She's just sitting next to the bed holding the hand of the arm that's still there.

Flicker is nine. She has five baby teeth left. She's missing an arm now. The AKC's official incident report calls it "an acceptable outcome given successful Fracture containment."

Acceptable.

December 25, 2019

The AKC posted a photo of us all in Santa hats. "Denver's defenders take a well-deserved holiday break! #KeeperChristmas #KeepGlowing"

Flicker has a prosthetic now. The AKC paid for it — it's a good one, way better than standard. She decorated it with stickers. She calls it her "robot arm." She's handling it better than any of us. She's 10 now. She went back to active deployment last month.

Brie gave me a book for Christmas — a collection of poetry. Mary Oliver. I didn't think I liked poetry but I read the whole thing in one night. There's a line that goes "what is it you plan to do with your one wild and precious life?" and I had to put the book down and stare at the ceiling for a while.

I called Mom. She picked up on the first ring. I think she waits by the phone on Christmas. We didn't talk about Keeper stuff. She asked me about books and if I was eating enough and whether I'd grown (I have — I'm taller than Maya now). She told me about her apartment in Portland and how it rains all the time and how she got a cat named Mochi.

She said "I love you, Sera." Not Starling. Sera.

I said it back.

February 18, 2020

I watched the Brightfall footage. I know I shouldn't have. Everyone says don't watch it. I watched it.

Keeper Brightfall. Bonded at 9, killed at 14, during a Class III in Chicago in 2017. Same year I bonded. She was my age when she died. Her death was the first one caught on live television.

The video is 47 seconds long. I won't describe what I saw. I will say that for the first 40 seconds she was still fighting. Her body was already failing and she was still fighting. She was trying to protect a group of civilians that were trapped behind a collapsed wall.

40 million views. The family asked for the video to be taken down. It's still everywhere.

I closed my laptop and sat in the dark for a long time. I could hear Flicker laughing in the common room. She was playing a card game with one of the new kids. She's learned to shuffle with one hand. She's adapted. That's what they say about Keepers — we adapt. We're resilient.

I'm so tired of the word resilient.

August 9, 2020

They did a magazine piece on me. "Starling: Denver's Invisible Guardian." Four-page spread in Keeper Weekly (which is a real publication that exists because of course it does). The photos are gorgeous. They shot me on the roof of the Denver Art Museum at golden hour, transformed, with my light constructs making these huge prismatic wings behind me. They asked me what I want to be when I grow up.

The interviewer caught themselves immediately — "I mean, what are your goals going forward" — but the damage was done. We both knew what the real answer was. I'm not going to grow up. Not the way she means. My goals going forward are to not die this year.

I smiled and said "I just want to keep keeping!" and they loved it. Great quote. Very on brand.

I am fourteen years old.

October 11, 2020

Class II. A deployment we'd done a hundred times. She was doing her speed sweep — the thing she always does, the thing she's the best in the district at. Tier 2 Hollow emerged at a junction point. She was moving too fast to redirect. She hit it at full speed.

Maya Chen, call sign "Quickstep." Bonded at 11, died at 16. Five years of service. 347 confirmed Hollow eliminations. Killed on a routine Class II in Commerce City, Colorado, on September 29th, 2020, at approximately 3:47 PM.

I was there. I was cloaking the south perimeter. I heard it happen. By the time I dropped cloak and reached her there was nothing to reach.

The AKC posted a memorial. "We honor the extraordinary service and sacrifice of Keeper Quickstep, who gave everything in defense of her community. Her light will never be forgotten. #RestInRadiance #ForeverQuickstep"

It got 2 million likes.

Brie held me while I screamed. We were in the bathroom at the academy because there's nowhere private in this place. I screamed until my voice gave out and then I just shook. Brie didn't say anything. She just held on.

Maya's parents came to collect her things. Her mom recognized me — I'd been to their house for dinner, once, a lifetime ago. She grabbed my hands and said "please be careful, Sera" and I said "I will" and we both knew I was lying.

I'm wearing the silver starling pendant Maya gave me. I haven't taken it off since she died. I'm never taking it off.

I'm 14. Maya was 16. She was supposed to have more time. Everyone always says "she was supposed to have more time" but what time? What time was she supposed to have? The median is 7 years and she got 5. There was never going to be enough time. There was never going to be a version of this where Maya got old.

I hate this. I hate this. I hate this.

January 30, 2021

New Keeper arrived at the academy today. Her name is Bethany, call sign "Prism." She's 11. Her ability is light-based, like mine — she can split Radiance into component wavelengths and use each one independently. The AKC is excited about the "synergy potential" of pairing us.

She looked at me with huge eyes and said "you're Starling! I had your poster on my wall!"

I had my poster on her wall. She is a child. She had my poster on her wall. And now she is here, in this place where children come to learn how to fight things that will eventually kill them, and she thinks it's an adventure.

I was nice to her. I showed her around. I introduced her to Flicker and Brie. I answered her questions and I smiled and I was warm and encouraging because what else am I supposed to do? Tell her the truth? She's 11. She's bonded. The truth won't un-bond her. It'll just take the only good part away early.

Agent Morales caught my eye over Bethany's head. We shared a look. Agent Morales has been doing this for eight years. She's had eleven Keepers. Four of them are dead. I'm the second-longest-surviving on her roster.

We don't talk about that. We share looks.

June 19, 2021

I can turn completely invisible now. Not just myself — I can cloak a 40-meter radius. Everything inside it disappears from visual, infrared, and electromagnetic detection. The AKC R&D people are losing their minds. They keep running tests. "Starling, can you extend to 50 meters?" "Starling, can you maintain it while moving?" "Starling, how long can you hold it?"

I held full cloak for 90 minutes during the last test. When I dropped it I had a nosebleed that lasted twenty minutes and I couldn't see straight for an hour.

They were thrilled.

I'm getting better, which means I'm getting used harder, which means I'm burning faster. I know this. They know this. Nobody says it because saying it would mean acknowledging that the AKC's deployment model is designed to use Keepers at the maximum rate their bodies can sustain, and the fact that "maximum rate" and "sustainable rate" are not the same thing is treated as an engineering problem rather than a moral one.

My hair is doing a thing. Just a few strands near my left temple. They've gone white. I'm 15.

November 2, 2021

Dawnchaser is dead.

Everyone knew it was coming. She'd been in burnout for over a year. The fan accounts documented the whole thing — the glow that wouldn't stop, the familiars manifesting on their own, the way she got thinner and brighter at the same time. There's a photograph of her from last month that went everywhere: sitting in a hospital bed, made of light, too bright to look at directly.

She was 19. She'd been a Keeper for 8 years. Her ability was so beautiful — these luminous animals that fought for her, like she had her own pack of light-wolves and light-eagles. Fans loved it. Kids wanted to be her.

The Radiance ate her alive. That's what burnout is. The power decides it wants more of you than you have left to give, so it takes everything.

The AKC classified her death as "natural consequence of extended Radiance integration." NATURAL CONSEQUENCE. Like it was weather. Like it was something that just happened instead of something we did to her by deploying her 200+ hours a year for nearly a decade because she was too good to bench.

I didn't know her personally. We were in different districts. But I knew her the way all Keepers know each other — the way soldiers know soldiers. She was one of us and now she's a memorial hashtag and a trending topic and a cautionary tale that nobody in charge will learn from.

I have white streaks in my hair. I'm 16.

March 29, 2022

There's a girl on Reddit. RadiantDusk_2019. She says she's Keeper Eventide, Seattle district — shadow manipulation, been active since 2016. She's been posting about deployment conditions, PTSD, the institutional neglect, all of it. Raw. Unflinching. The most honest thing I've ever read about what it's like to be us.

I made a throwaway account and DMed her. I said "I'm a Keeper. I won't say who. Everything you're writing is true. Thank you."

She wrote back: "I know it's true. That's why they'll never respond to it. Stay safe out there."

Stay safe. As if that's something we get to do.

October 14, 2022

My birthday. 17. Brie organized a small party — just our squad, Flicker, Prism, and the newer kids. We had cake. Brie made me a bird out of her iron-Radiance, like she did when I was 11. This one was more detailed. It had individual feathers. Five years of practice.

Brie is 20. She's been a Keeper for 10 years. She is one of the oldest active Keepers in the AKC. Her limp is worse. She has arthritis in her hands — her HANDS, she's TWENTY — from years of channeling Radiance through iron constructs. Some mornings she can't close her fists until she runs them under hot water for ten minutes.

She doesn't complain. She never complains. She just quietly runs her hands under hot water and then goes to training and then goes to deployment and then comes back and does it again.

I asked her once if she was scared. She said, "I was scared from 10 to about 14. Then I was angry from 14 to 17. Now I'm just here."

Just here. Ten years of fighting and dying and watching people die and she's "just here."

Happy birthday to me.

October 22, 2022

Portland.

I wasn't there. Denver wasn't deployed. But we watched the feeds. Everyone watched the feeds.

Class IV. The first one in years. The Fracture ripped open six blocks of downtown and Hollow poured out for hours. They deployed everyone they had. It wasn't enough.

847 civilians dead. Three Keepers dead.

Gemini and Parallax. The twins. 15 years old. They requested to be deployed together. The AKC granted the request.

I keep thinking about that. They REQUESTED it. They wanted to fight side by side. Because that's what we're trained to want — to be brave, to be heroes, to stand together against the dark. And the system said yes because of course it said yes, it always says yes, it has never once said "no, you are children, stay home."

The third Keeper who died was Keeper Basalt, call sign I don't remember, age 17. I can't find much about him — her — I can't even remember. That's awful. Three people died and I can't even remember the third one's details because the twins' story is so much more dramatic and that's exactly the problem, isn't it? We mourn in hierarchies. Some deaths are more photogenic than others.

1,200+ names on the wall now. And counting.

February 5, 2023

I'm 17 and I am one of the most senior Keepers in the Denver district.

Let me say that again. I am SEVENTEEN and I am one of the most SENIOR Keepers in my DISTRICT. I have been doing this for five and a half years. Most of the girls I started with are dead. The ones who aren't dead are hurt or burning. I am the veteran. I am the Brie. The new kids look at me the way I used to look at Brie — like I have answers, like I know something, like surviving this long means I've figured out some secret.

I haven't figured out anything. I'm just lucky. Luck and a useful ability — invisibility keeps you alive longer than frontline powers do because you're not the one getting hit. That's not wisdom. That's geometry.

The new class is so young. There's a girl who bonded at 8. EIGHT. She's in third grade. She still has a gap where her front teeth are growing in. She asked me to help her with her light constructs because she heard I'm "the light girl" and she can do a little bit of light stuff too. So I sat with an eight-year-old and taught her how to focus Radiance into a beam and I smiled and was patient and encouraging and inside I was screaming so loud I thought my teeth would crack.

July 17, 2023

New director. Katherine Holt. Career DoD. Ran drone logistics before this.

Drone. Logistics.

The person in charge of a program for children who fight and die was previously responsible for optimizing the deployment patterns of unmanned aircraft. I'm sure she sees no meaningful difference between the two roles.

Within her first month she's already talking about "increased operational tempo" and "asset utilization efficiency." Asset utilization efficiency. That's us. We're the assets.

Brie laughed when I told her. Not a good laugh. The kind of laugh that means you've run out of other responses.

October 14, 2023

18.

Brie made me a bird. She always makes me a bird. This one was smaller than last year's — her hands are getting worse. She tried to hide that she was in pain while shaping it. I pretended not to notice.

She's 21. Eleven years. She shouldn't be alive. Neither should I. Neither should any of us.

I didn't want a party this year. I asked for the day off deployment. The AKC denied the request. Class I in Thornton. Routine. In and out in two hours. Happy birthday, Starling. Go kill some monsters and come back in time for cake.

I went. I killed some monsters. I came back.

Flicker saved me a slice. She's 14 now. Her prosthetic arm is covered in doodles — she lets the younger kids draw on it with markers. She is, impossibly, one of the most well-adjusted people I know. I don't understand how. Maybe losing part of yourself early teaches you something about what's left. Or maybe she's just built different, as the fans would say. The fans love saying that.

I have more white in my hair. It's visible now — a streak from my left temple that I can't hide anymore. The fan accounts have noticed. They think it makes me look "badass." One account posted a side-by-side of me at 11 and me at 18 with the caption "glow-up of the century." In the first photo I'm grinning with my whole face. In the second one I'm not.

January 3, 2024

Prism is dead.

Bethany Okafor. Call sign "Prism." Age 14. Bonded at 11. Three years of service.

Class II deployment. She split a beam wrong — sent the wrong wavelength at a Tier 2 and it passed right through. The Hollow reached her before anyone could intervene.

She had my poster on her wall. She came to the academy with stars in her eyes and I showed her around and I was kind to her and I trained with her and I watched her grow from a scared kid into a competent Keeper and now she is dead at 14 and I am writing about it in a notebook because there is nowhere else to put it.

Bethany never mentioned it. She never used it for special treatment or protection. She was just a kid who wanted to help.

The AKC sent flowers to the family. They sent a flag and a certificate and $47,000. The same $47,000 they've been sending since 2014. Not adjusted for inflation. Her life, in the government's accounting, is worth less every year.

I am not okay.

March 19, 2024

I stopped giving interviews. The AKC PR team is not happy about it. They say Starling has "significant brand value" and my "engagement metrics" are important for "public confidence in the Keeper program."

Brand. Value. Engagement. Metrics. Public. Confidence.

A child I trained is dead and they want me to smile for cameras.

Agent Morales backed me up. She told them I was in a "recovery rotation" and needed space. That bought me a few weeks. She's risking her position for me. She's been doing that more and more — small rebellions, quiet pushbacks, reports filed just a little late so that certain deployment orders get delayed just long enough for a Keeper to get an extra hour of rest.

She can't save us. She knows she can't save us. But she's trying to slow down the rate at which the machine grinds through us, and I think that's the bravest thing I've ever seen.

August 8, 2024

Brie is in burnout.

She told me yesterday. Calmly. Like she was reporting the weather. "My transformation times are slowing down. The iron's coming out brittle. Morales noticed. I noticed before she did."

She's 22. Twelve years of service. The longest-active Keeper in Denver history.

I asked her what the AKC medical team said. She laughed — that laugh again — and said they'd "noted the decline in output metrics" and recommended she "moderate her exertion during high-intensity deployments." Moderate her exertion. During combat with extradimensional kill-beasts. Sure. Just, like, fight the monsters gently.

They haven't reduced her deployment schedule. Of course they haven't. There aren't enough of us. There are never enough of us.

I sat with her on the roof of the academy and we watched the sun set over the Rockies and she said, "You know the worst part? I still love it. I still love the moment when the iron takes shape and I'm holding something I made out of nothing and the light hits it and it's beautiful. Even now. Even knowing what it's doing to me. I still love it."

I couldn't say anything. I held her hand — carefully, because her fingers don't close all the way anymore — and we watched the sky change colors and I bent the light a little, just a little, so the sunset lasted a few minutes longer for us.

October 14, 2024

19.

No bird from Brie this year. She can't shape the fine details anymore. Her hands shake. She apologized and I told her to shut up and I hugged her and we both pretended we weren't crying.

I am 19 years old. I have been a Keeper for 7 years. I am at the median. Statistically, I am already supposed to be dead. Every day from now on is borrowed.

Legally, I'm an adult. I can vote and sign contracts and join the military for real and do all the things adults do. But I've been fighting a war since I was 11. I've been killing since I was 11. I've been watching my friends die since I was 14.

Flicker bought me cupcakes. She's 15, missing an arm, and she bought me cupcakes with little star sprinkles and I ate them and they were good and I am so tired.

I called Mom. She didn't pick up. I called again. She picked up on the second try and her voice was hoarse and I think she'd been crying before she answered. We talked for an hour. She told me about Mochi the cat and about her garden and about a book she read that she thought I'd like. She didn't ask about deployments. She never asks about deployments.

At the end she said "please come visit me in Portland, Sera."

I said "I will, Mom. Soon."

We both know I won't. I can't. The Radiance pulls. The Fractures don't stop. And Portland is where the Class IV happened and I don't think I could stand in that city and feel the ghosts.

February 15, 2025

Brie died this morning.

Not in combat. In her bed. In the academy. The Radiance — it just — she was glowing. When they found her she was glowing so bright they couldn't enter the room. The light burned. Not a metaphor. It burned. Radiance pouring out of her like she was a sun and her body couldn't contain it anymore.

By the time the light faded there was nothing left that looked like Brie. Just light, and then not even that.

Bridget Vasquez. Call sign "Ironveil." Bonded at 10, died at 22. Twelve years of service. 1,200+ confirmed Hollow eliminations. Killed by the thing she used to save people.

She made me a bird the day I arrived. A little iron bird that glowed in the dark. I still have it. It's on my nightstand. I'm looking at it right now. It's the only thing she ever made that the Radiance didn't take back.

I am not writing anything inspirational here. I am not finding a silver lining. She was my friend and my sister and the first person who was kind to me in this place and she is gone because the system used her until there was nothing left and then the Radiance did what the Radiance does and finished the job.

She was 22 and she had arthritis and white hair and she couldn't close her hands and she still made me birds every year because she loved me and now she's dead and I'm holding an iron bird and I can't breathe.

Seven Eight years I've been a Keeper. The longest-serving in Denver now.

That's not an honor. It's a countdown.

April 22, 2025

The white in my hair is spreading. Both temples now, threading back. My nosebleeds are more frequent — three this week, one during a deployment. I was cloaking a civilian corridor during a Class II and my nose just started pouring blood. I held the cloak anyway because there were people behind me and they would have died if the Hollow saw them. I held it for twenty minutes with blood running down my face and soaking into my uniform.

Afterward, Agent Morales looked at me and I looked at her and neither of us said the word burnout.

My transformation is slower. Half a second — maybe a full second — longer than it used to be. Nobody would notice who wasn't looking for it. I'm looking for it.

I requested a meeting with the AKC medical team. They ran tests. "Elevated Radiance cycling markers." "Consistent with extended service duration." "Recommend continued monitoring."

Continued monitoring. They're going to watch me die the way they watched Brie die and Dawnchaser die and they're going to write it down in a file and call it a natural consequence.

I am 19.

June 30, 2025

Flicker asked me today if I'm okay.

Not in the general way people ask it. She asked it the way someone asks when they already know the answer but need to hear you say it. She's 16 now. She's not a kid anymore. She's a veteran, like me, like I was at her age, and she can see what I see in the mirror every morning.

I said "I'm fine, Flick."

She said "You're doing the Brie thing."

I asked what the Brie thing was.

"Being okay so hard that everyone can tell you're not."

I didn't have an answer for that.

She sat next to me and we were quiet for a while and then she said, "I'm going to make it to 20. I decided." Like it was a choice. Like willpower meant anything against the math.

I said, "Yeah, you are."

She said, "You too."

I said, "Yeah. Me too."

September 3, 2025

I caught a new recruit staring at the Memorial Wall display in the academy lobby — we don't have the real wall, just a screen that scrolls the names. She was reading them. All 1,847 of them. She was 10, maybe 11, freshly bonded, still in civilian clothes. She turned to me and said, "Are all of these Keepers who died?"

I said yes.

She said, "But there are so many."

I said yes.

She said, "Am I going to be on that wall?"

I should have lied. The AKC would have wanted me to lie. The media coaching would have had me say something about how we all do our best and the important thing is the people we protect and every Keeper's sacrifice is honored and—

I said, "Probably."

She started crying. I sat down next to her and put my arm around her and she cried into my shoulder and I thought: this is the first honest thing anyone in this building has said to a new Keeper in the entire time I've been here.

Agent Morales found us. She didn't reprimand me. She sat on the girl's other side and put her arm around her too. Three people on the floor of the lobby, under a screen full of dead children's names.

I'm going to be on that wall. Not probably. Definitely. It's just a question of when.

September 29, 2025

Five years since Maya died. September 29th, 2020. I wore the silver starling pendant all day, the way I always do, but today I held it in my hand during the morning briefing and nobody asked why.

The newer kids don't know who Maya was. Why would they? She's a name on a screen. One of 1,847. She was my best friend and she was the fastest person I've ever seen and she bought me a silver bird and she's been dead longer than some of these kids have been Keepers.

I went to the roof alone tonight. I made lights in the sky — a murmuration, like starlings, like the real birds. Thousands of little points of light, swooping and diving and folding over each other. I used to do this for fun when I was 11. I did it because it was beautiful and I could.

It's still beautiful. My nose bled the whole time.

October 14, 2025

20.

Flicker made me cupcakes again. Star sprinkles again. She's 16 and she's my best friend now, the way Maya was my best friend, and I see her and I love her and I am terrified because I know what happens to people I love in this place.

Agent Morales gave me a card. She wrote in it: "Eight years. I don't have the words. — M."

She doesn't have the words because there are no words for this. What do you say to a 20-year-old who's been fighting since she was 11? Congratulations on continuing to not be dead? Well done on another year of state-sanctioned child labor? Happy birthday, asset, your utilization metrics remain within acceptable parameters?

I have a fan wiki page. It says my birthday is October 14th. It says my ability is light refraction. It says my status is "Active." Someday — not long from now — someone will change that word to "Deceased" and that will be the entirety of the transition. One word. Active to Deceased. A toggle. A field in a database.

There will be memorial posts. Black profile pictures. Hashtags. #RestInRadiance #ForeverStarling. Someone will make a compilation video of my best deployment footage set to a sad song. People will cry over it. They'll say I was brave. They'll say I shone so bright.

And then there will be a new girl with a new ability and a new call sign and new fan art and they'll shine too, for a while, until they don't.

November 18, 2025

Class III tomorrow. Downtown Denver. The Fracture opened at 9 PM tonight and it's been expanding for three hours. Already Class II, upgrading to III. Full district deployment at dawn.

The team is me, Flicker, two Keepers from the Colorado Springs office I've worked with before, and a 12-year-old named Keeper "Halo" who has been active for four months. Four months.

I am the senior Keeper. I am the one they look to. I am the Brie.

I spent the last hour running through tactics with the team. I assigned positions. I put Halo on rear support with strict instructions to stay behind Flicker's burst perimeter. I put myself on optical cover — full cloak over the civilian corridor. It's what I'm best at. It's what burns me fastest.

Agent Morales pulled me aside after the briefing. She didn't say anything for a minute. Then she said, "Your nosebleed during the last deployment lasted forty minutes, Sera."

I said, "I know."

She said, "You don't have to take the forward cloak position. You could run support. Let someone else—"

I said, "There is no one else. You know that."

She knows that. I'm the only one who can cloak a full evacuation corridor. If I don't do it, civilians die. That's the math. That's always the math. That's how they keep us — not with chains, but with math. The equation always comes out the same: your body or their bodies. And we always choose theirs because that's what they raised us to do.

She looked at me for a long time. I thought she might cry. She didn't. She's a professional. She's buried too many of us to cry at the living.

She said, "Come back, Sera."

I said, "I'll try."

I'm sitting on my bed now. It's 1 AM. The deployment is at 6. I should sleep. I won't.

I'm holding the iron bird. Brie's bird. It still glows, faintly, in the dark. Whatever Radiance she put into it is still there, years later. The only part of her that lasted. I wonder if that means something. I wonder if there's a part of us that outlasts the burning. Probably not. Probably it's just metal.

I'm 20 years old. I've been a Keeper for 8 years. I have white in my hair and blood in my nose and light leaking from my hands when I sleep. I have a list of dead friends I recite like a prayer: Maya, Brie, Prism, and all the others — there are so many others. I have a mom in Portland who I haven't seen in three years and a cat named Mochi I've never met. I have a silver bird around my neck and an iron bird in my hand and a Core in my chest that will never, ever let me go.

I used to make murmurations on my ceiling. Little lights, swooping and diving. I did it because it was beautiful. I did it because I could.

I'll do it now. One more time. Little lights in the dark.

Tomorrow I go back out.
